## 3. Honest assessment

Under Direction B (started 2026-07-13, after RYDC Arm A returned a strong null), roughly 19–20 new hypotheses were tested against the pre-registered gate stack. **Zero cleared all gates.** Notably:

- The failure modes are *varied and mechanism-appropriate*, not one gate mechanically rejecting everything the same way: outright statistical nulls, negative Sharpe, one underpowered-not-falsified case, one batch caught specifically by label-shuffle (exactly the overfitting-detection mechanism it exists for), and one near-miss (WS-A TSMOM) caught specifically by PBO despite a superficially attractive DSR p-value.
- No hypothesis was promoted on a favorable single metric while other gates disagreed — the WS-A TSMOM case in particular shows the gate stack doing its job: DSR alone would have looked like a pass, PBO=1.0 correctly vetoed it.
- The sacred holdout (`data/sacred_holdout/holdout.csv`) remains untouched (`use_count: 0`) — no hypothesis got far enough to warrant burning it, which is itself informative: nothing survived even the pre-holdout gate stack.

This reads as a research pipeline that is **doing what it was built to do** (rejecting overfit/underpowered/null results correctly) rather than a broken or under-tested one. The absence of a passing hypothesis after ~19 well-varied attempts (technical, cross-asset macro, session/ICT patterns, vol clustering, momentum) is itself evidence, per the rule's own framing in §3.5: *"System is correctly built; edge does not exist (or is not accessible with public data + current methodology)."*

Evidence favors **Option A — stop completely**, on these grounds:

2. The single closest-to-passing result (WS-A TSMOM) failed on the primary HAC gate and showed near-certain overfitting on PBO — not a near-miss worth one more iteration, a rejection the gate stack is designed to produce.
